NextGen: Lucky-Number Prize Draw Platform

Sandbox demo, no real moneyFintech

A real-time digital draw platform: players pick numbers, join a pool, and a provably fair draw chooses the grand-prize winner and cash-prize winners, with a wallet, KYC, responsible-gaming controls and a back office.

TypeProduct build
RoleArchitecture & build
StackDjango · PostgreSQL
StatusSandbox demo

The problem

A prize-draw platform handles other people's money, so it has to prove three things: the draw is fair, every naira is accounted for, and players are protected. NextGen was designed around those three requirements first.

How it works

  • Play: players buy coins (₦10 per coin by default) and spend 50 coins per entry; one tap gives four lucky numbers. Two games run every day.
  • Prizes: a configurable share of each batch's sales funds one grand prize for the closest ticket, then ₦7,000 cash prizes for the next closest.
  • Winnings: prizes go into a separate naira Winnings balance, the only balance that can be withdrawn.
  • Scheduler: closes sales, draws the numbers, settles winners and opens the next draw automatically.

Fairness, money and safety

  • Provably fair RNG: when a draw opens, the SHA-256 hash of a secret server seed is published; after the draw, the seed is revealed so anyone can check that the result wasn't changed.
  • Double-entry wallet ledger: every movement is a balanced pair of entries. On PostgreSQL a database trigger makes ledger rows physically impossible to update or delete.
  • Payments: deposits and payouts through Nigerian payment providers, behind a gateway interface with a sandbox implementation.
  • Compliance: tiered KYC (with ID upload), responsible-gaming limits and self-exclusion, and a back office for operations with role-based access.
  • Tests: 123 automated tests, plus a ledger-integrity check command.

Status: the public demo runs in sandbox mode: mock payment gateway and KYC, with a “no real money” banner on every page. Going live would need a licence, real payment-provider keys and an always-on background worker.

What it shows

  • Financial data integrity (immutable ledger, balanced entries).
  • Verifiable randomness and auditability.
  • Designing for regulation from the start: KYC, limits, audit trail.
